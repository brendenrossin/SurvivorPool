# GroupMe weekly recap bot — design spec

**Status:** design approved 2026-09-05 by the owner ("yup go for it we can try it out")
**Branch:** `feature/groupme-recap` (from `staging` @ `fd84791`)
**Touches:** new `api/groupme.py`, new `api/recap/` package, new `jobs/ingest_groupme.py`,
new `jobs/backfill_groupme.py`, new `scripts/recap_backtest.py`, two new tables in
`api/models.py`, and a trigger hook in `jobs/update_scores.py`

First LLM dependency in the repo — there is no `anthropic`, `openai`, or any other
provider in `requirements.txt` today. Also the first outbound integration: every
existing job reads from somewhere and writes to Postgres. This one writes to a place
real people are reading.

---

## Why

The pool already has a chat. Entrants post picks to a GroupMe, argue there, and the
manager aggregates from it into the Google Sheet (`docs/pool-process.md`). The
dashboard is a second destination nobody is already looking at. Rather than asking the
pool to come to the dashboard for interactivity, the dashboard goes to them: after
every game in a week is final, a bot posts one short recap into the chat.

This is section 7 of `docs/survivor_pool_product_roadmap.md` — "Discord Integration
for Notifications & Messaging" — with the platform corrected to the one the pool
actually uses. That section is otherwise unchanged in intent.

Inbound (mirroring GroupMe onto the dashboard as a live message board) was considered
and **rejected for now**, for two reasons recorded here so it isn't relitigated:

- The GroupMe is where picks are posted *before* kickoff. Mirroring it to the
  dashboard would republish future weeks' picks to the dashboard audience, which is
  the exact disclosure `should_reveal_picks` and the grid's week clamp exist to
  prevent. See the correction note in the roadmap's Dashboard & Platform section.
- The dashboard has no identity layer — no user table, no login. Open posting from a
  public Railway URL into a private group chat is an unauthenticated write relay.
  Revisit after Phase 5 (`In-App Pick Submission`) lands auth.

Reading GroupMe is still in scope, but only as **input to the recap**, never as
dashboard output.

## The design problem: "big week" is two axes, not one

The owner named this before any data was pulled:

> "big loser weeks would likely be based off a semantic idea of how big the chopping
> block is, like 5/15 surviving in later weeks would be big but also 350/400 surviging
> would still be big in earlier weeks based on raw numbers alone"

The 2025 season in the staging DB confirms it. Read-only query against
`mainline.proxy.rlwy.net`, 252 players, 1,379 picks, weeks 1–14:

| wk | alive at start | eliminated | rate |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 252 | 6 | 2.4% |
| 2 | 246 | 8 | 3.3% |
| **3** | 238 | **66** | 27.7% |
| 4 | 172 | 43 | 25.0% |
| **5** | 129 | **53** | **41.1%** |
| 6 | 76 | 13 | 17.1% |
| **7** | 63 | **1** | 1.6% |
| 8 | 62 | 21 | 33.9% |
| 9 | 41 | 7 | 17.1% |
| 10 | 34 | 10 | 29.4% |
| **11** | 24 | **0** | **0.0%** |
| 12 | 24 | 0 | 0.0% |
| 13 | 24 | 2 | 8.3% |
| **14** | 22 | **18** | **81.8%** |

Week 3 killed 66 people at a 27.7% rate. Week 14 killed 18 at 81.8%. **A single
scalar ranks those two in opposite orders depending on which axis it encodes.** Any
design that reduces the week to one "carnage score" has to pick which of those weeks
is the bigger story, and both answers are defensible — which is the tell that it is
the wrong shape.

**Resolution: no scalar, and no hard-coded tone thresholds.** `features.py` computes a
feature vector deterministically; the prompt carries a rubric; the model picks the
angle. Thresholds are what the backtest would otherwise spend all its time fighting.

### The fourth scenario nobody listed

Weeks 11 and 12 of 2025 had **zero** eliminations. Twice in a season, nothing happens.
A recap generated by the same machinery that handles week 14 will be limp. The owner
chose "post something short" over silence, so a quiet week is a first-class scenario
with its own few-shot set, not a degraded normal week.

Scenario set, anchored to real 2025 weeks:

| Scenario | Anchor week | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Massacre by rate | 14 | 18 of 22, pool-ending stakes |
| Body count, moderate rate | 3 | 66 of 238 |
| Big on both axes | 5 | 53 of 129 |
| Barely anything | 7 | 1 of 63 |
| Nobody died | 11 | 0 of 24 |

## Not sounding AI generated

This is the hardest requirement and it is a prompting problem, not an architecture
problem. Measures in descending order of how much they actually matter:

**1. The 280-character cap, enforced in code.** Most AI tells are *length* tells: the
throat-clearing open, the tricolon, "it's not just X, it's Y," the summarizing
sign-off. At tweet length there is no room for any of them. Generation validates
length and retries once on overflow rather than truncating mid-sentence.

The failure mode inverts at this length — the risk stops being bloat and becomes a
flat stat line. The prompt's instruction is therefore "have one angle, land it, stop,"
not "be concise."

**2. Few-shot from the group's own liked messages.** This is the single largest lever
on voice, and the reason the read integration earns its place. Examples are labeled as
**voice reference** — register, rhythm, profanity level, capitalisation habits — with
an explicit instruction not to reuse their content and not to reach for callbacks. The
owner's "only if the bit fits" is honored by not letting the model hunt for bits at
all; a callback that survives is one that arrived on its own.

**3. A ban list — emojis included, and enforced in code.** Em-dashes, "Let's",
tricolons, sign-off lines, "not just X, it's Y," and **no emojis at all**.

Emojis are banned in the prompt *and* validated on the way out, alongside the
character cap — a prompt instruction alone leaks one through eventually, and one 🔥 at
the end of a clause undoes every other measure in this section. Note the corpus may
well be full of them, since real people use emojis freely; the ban is on the bot's own
output only, and the mismatch is deliberate. LLM emoji use is decorative and
end-of-clause, which reads differently from how the group uses them.

**4. Permission to be mean.** Survivor pool chat is mean. A polite recap is an AI tell
by itself.

### The meme is code's decision, not the model's

The pool has a running bit: when Travis removes people from the GroupMe after their
team loses, someone posts a Kylo Ren gif. The gif is uploaded once to GroupMe's image
service and fires on a **deterministic** condition in `api/recap/memes.py` — an
elimination-rate threshold, or observed removals in the chat.

A running bit is funny because it is reliable. A model deciding when to be funny
produces a meme that does not land, and produces it at unpredictable times, which is
the opposite of a bit.

## Point-in-time replay

The owner caught the methodological hole: a backtest that builds its voice corpus from
the whole chat history is reading messages that did not exist when the recap would
have posted, and will overstate quality.

`build_voice_corpus(db, as_of)` filters `created_at < as_of`. For week W, `as_of` is
the moment the last game of W went final — the same condition that fires the trigger in
production. `games` does not store a "went final" timestamp, so this is derived as
`max(kickoff) for the week + 3.5h`.

**Known limitation, not closeable:** GroupMe returns *current* favorite counts, with no
per-like timestamps. So `created_at` filtering is clean, but ranking the corpus by
likes still peeks at the future — a week-3 message may have collected its likes in week
8. Likes in a group chat mostly land within hours of posting, so the leak is mild, but
the backtest is "near point-in-time," not pure, and results should be read that way.

## Components

Follows the existing split: `api/` holds providers and domain logic alongside
`score_providers.py` / `odds_providers.py` / `sheets.py`, `jobs/` holds workers.
Nothing lands in `app/`, which is Streamlit view code only.

```
api/groupme.py             fetch_messages / post_message / upload_image
api/recap/features.py      build_week_features(db, season, week) -> WeekFeatures
api/recap/voice.py         build_voice_corpus(db, as_of, n) -> list[str]
api/recap/generate.py      generate_recap(features, corpus, scenario) -> str
api/recap/memes.py         deterministic trigger -> pre-uploaded image URL
jobs/ingest_groupme.py     poller cron
jobs/backfill_groupme.py   one-time walk back to 2025 season start
scripts/recap_backtest.py  eval harness, always dry-run, never posts
```

`api/groupme.py` reuses `api/rate_limiter.py` (`APIRateLimiter.wait_if_needed()`)
rather than adding a second throttle.

### Endpoints

| Purpose | Call | Auth |
|---|---|---|
| Read messages | `GET /v3/groups/:id/messages` (`before_id` paginates backward) | user access token |
| Post | `POST /v3/bots/post` | `bot_id` only |
| Upload image | `POST` to GroupMe's image service | user access token |

A `bot_id` is bound to one group at creation. This matters — see Configuration.

### `WeekFeatures`

Deterministic, no network, no LLM, fully unit-testable. This is where TDD applies.

`week`, `alive_at_start`, `eliminated`, `survivors`, `elimination_rate`,
`pct_of_original_field_left`, `most_popular_pick_that_died` (+ count),
`biggest_favorite_that_lost` (+ spread), `scenario`.

`games.point_spread` and `games.favorite_team` already exist, so the upset field needs
no schema change.

## Data model

**`chat_messages`** — `message_id` (GroupMe's, PK), `group_id`, `sender_id`,
`sender_name`, `sender_type`, `text`, `favorite_count`, `is_system`, `created_at`.

The poller re-scans a trailing ~2-week window on every run. Favorite counts grow after
a message posts, so insert-once would permanently undercount exactly the material the
corpus is built from.

Corpus filters: `is_system = false` (excludes "X removed Y from the group"),
`sender_type != 'bot'`, `favorite_count >= 1`, non-empty text.

**`weekly_recaps`** — `season`, `week` (**unique together**), `body_text`, `model`,
`prompt_version`, `features_json`, `meme_url`, `posted_at`, `groupme_message_id`,
`dry_run`.

The unique constraint is the idempotency guard. `update_scores.py` runs hourly on
Sundays, so the "all games final" trigger fires repeatedly and must post exactly once.
`features_json` and `prompt_version` are stored so a past recap can be explained
without re-deriving state that has since changed.

## Model and cost

The owner asked: *"maybe we use haiku or sonnet or something cheap?"*

At tweet length the workload is ~2K input tokens (voice corpus + features) and ~80
output tokens. Per recap, and per full 18-week season:

| Model | Per recap | Season |
|---|---|---|
| `claude-opus-5` | $0.012 | **$0.22** |
| `claude-sonnet-5` | $0.005 | $0.09 |
| `claude-haiku-4-5` | $0.002 | $0.04 |

Cheap-versus-expensive is an 18-cent decision across an entire season. Against that,
voice imitation is the task class small models are worst at, and "must not sound AI
generated" is the headline requirement — so there is real tension between the two
asks.

**This spec does not resolve it by argument.** Phase 3 runs all three models across all
five anchor weeks (15 generations, ~$0.10) and the owner reads the output. Default is
`claude-opus-5` with adaptive thinking at `effort: medium` unless the bake-off says
otherwise; `RECAP_MODEL` stays an env var so it can change without a deploy.

**No prompt caching.** It runs weekly. The cache TTL is 5 minutes by default and 1 hour
at most, so the hit rate is structurally zero. Adding it would be cargo cult.

## Configuration

| Var | staging | production |
|---|---|---|
| `GROUPME_ACCESS_TOKEN` | same | same |
| `GROUPME_READ_GROUP_ID` | **real chat** | real chat |
| `GROUPME_BOT_ID` | **test group's bot** | real chat's bot |
| `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` | ✓ | ✓ |
| `RECAP_MODEL` | tunable | tunable |
| `RECAP_DRY_RUN` | default `true` | `false` |

Staging reads the **real** chat, because a voice test against a corpus of test messages
tests nothing. Reading cannot annoy anyone.

Staging posts to a **separate** group. Because a `bot_id` is bound to its group at
creation, staging's bot has no code path that reaches the real chat — not with a bad
config, not with a wrong `ENVIRONMENT` value. That is a stronger guarantee than a
runtime flag, and `RECAP_DRY_RUN` sits on top of it rather than replacing it.

Per the environment notes in `CLAUDE.md`, these must be set on the service that runs
scores in each environment (`Scores-Cron` / `Scores-Cron-Prod`), and the CLI can `--set`
but cannot unset.

## Build order

Each phase is independently verifiable, so work can stop at any boundary.

1. **GroupMe read.** `api/groupme.py`, `chat_messages`, `jobs/ingest_groupme.py`,
   backfill to the 2025 season start. Verifiable: real messages in the DB with
   favorite counts.
2. **Features + harness, no LLM.** `features.py` and `scripts/recap_backtest.py`
   printing feature vectors for weeks 3, 5, 7, 11, 14. Verifiable against the table
   above.
3. **Generation + bake-off.** Three models × five weeks, dry-run. Owner reads output;
   prompt and model are tuned here.
4. **Post to the staging test group.** `RECAP_DRY_RUN=false` in staging only. Soak.
5. **Production bot.**

Phase 4 is blocked on the owner creating the test group and both bots — an
out-of-repo prerequisite, not a code task.

## Open questions

- **GroupMe size is unknown.** Backfilling a year of a chat for a 252-player pool at
  100 messages per request could be thousands of calls, which shapes phase 1's
  throttling and runtime. Measure before writing the backfill loop.
- **Whether all 252 players are in the chat**, or the GroupMe covers a subset of the
  pool. Affects how representative the voice corpus is.
- **Recap on the dashboard.** Storing `weekly_recaps` makes rendering past recaps on
  the dashboard nearly free later. Deliberately out of scope for this spec.
